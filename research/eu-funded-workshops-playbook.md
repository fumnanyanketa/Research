# From Participant to Organizer: How to Run EU-Funded Workshops

**Research report · 29 August 2026 · Status of all facts verified as of this date**

Prepared for Chukwufumnanya Nketa (Y-tunnus 3612302-9), Espoo, in response to the de minimis
notification received from the Tekoälyhautomo project. The question answered here: *how do I become
the organization that runs EU-funded AI literacy workshops — where the EU pays so participants attend
free, including paid access to the AI tools and tokens used in training?*

---

## 1. TL;DR

**Yes, this is a real, repeatable model, and you can get into it.** The letter you received is the
visible tip of a well-documented machine: an organization wins an EU project grant, delivers free
workshops to companies, values each free seat at market price (yours: €400), and books that value as
**de minimis state aid** to each participating company. The organizer gets ~60–75% of its project
costs paid by the EU + Finnish state; participation fees and own funds cover the rest.

Five things the research established:

1. **Your current business form is the first blocker.** A *toiminimi* (sole trader) is not a legal
   person and **cannot lead** an EU structural-fund project. A limited company (Oy) or a registered
   association (ry) can. Your fastest credible applicant vehicle may be **AFAES ry** — associations
   win these grants routinely, and community-focused skills projects fit them naturally.
2. **The fastest money is not your own EU project** — it's plugging into machinery that already has
   funding: subcontracting/partnering with the **FAIR EDIH** (Helsinki, AI-focused, renewed to
   ~2028, "mostly free services" delivered by its consortium), joining an AMK-led consortium as
   co-implementer, winning **employment-area training tenders**, and **Jotpa**-procured free courses
   (a private company, MinnaLearn, delivered Finland's national free AI courses — the precedent).
3. **Leading your own project is a 2027 move, not a 2026 one.** The directly relevant calls for
   Uusimaa (ESF+ "Uutta osaamista työelämään" €2M, EAKR urban development €4.5M) closed in
   March–April 2026. More calls are announced for 2026–2027 — the current programme period's last
   window, since projects must end by ~2028. Use the next 6–12 months to build references and
   relationships, then apply.
4. **Yes, project money can pay for AI subscriptions and tokens for participants** — but only under
   the right cost model. Under the default 40% flat-rate model, purchases are buried in the flat
   rate; under the **7% flat-rate model**, purchased services (tool subscriptions, API credits,
   external trainers, venues for the target group) are itemizable eligible costs. No Finnish project
   has yet itemized AI tokens as a de minimis benefit — you'd be an early mover, but the EDIH
   "price-list" method is the ready-made template.
5. **Two different games, know which you're playing.** Workshops for **companies** trigger the whole
   de minimis machinery. Workshops for **individuals** (your community program for immigrants) are
   **not state aid at all** — no €400 letters, no declarations — and are funded through easier
   channels (Jotpa, ESF+ participation calls, Erasmus+, city grants, adult-education partnerships).

## 2. What your letter actually is

The document is a **tiedoksianto** — a statutory notification of de minimis state aid — and decoding
it reveals the whole model:

| Field in your letter | Value | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|
| Programme | Uudistuva ja osaava Suomi 2021–2027 | Finland's €3.2bn EU regional & structural policy programme (ERDF + ESF+ + JTF) |
| Project / code | Tekoälyhautomo, A81221 | An EAKR (ERDF) group project, 1.9.2024–31.8.2027 |
| Beneficiary (tuensaaja) | Metropolia amk | The organizer role you want. Metropolia is one of five implementers |
| Granting authority | Uudenmaan liitto | Helsinki-Uusimaa Regional Council — the funder who formally "grants" your €400 |
| Benefit | Workshop participation fee, €400 | The market price the project assigned to your free seat |
| Legal basis | De minimis regulation | Free services to companies = state aid; under €300k/3yrs it needs no Commission approval |

**Anatomy of the project behind it** (from public decisions and project pages):

- **Tekoälyhautomo** is a *ryhmähanke* (group project): lead **Haaga-Helia**, co-implementers
  **Metropolia, City of Helsinki, City of Espoo, City of Vantaa**.
- Total budget **€1,485,972**, of which EU+state support **€891,583 = 60%**; the rest is the
  partners' own/municipal money. Metropolia's own slice: ~€165k.
- Funded from the **capital-region sustainable urban development (kestävä kaupunkikehittäminen)
  EAKR envelope** — a call that has run in 2022, 2024 and 2026 (€4.49M in the 1.2.–11.3.2026 round).
- What it delivers: **AIStart**, a free 7-week AI incubator for metropolitan micro/SMEs — nearly 80
  companies trained by mid-2026. Its predecessor **AI-TIE** (Haaga-Helia + Laurea, EAKR from
  Uudenmaan liitto) ran the same design. This is a **repeating pattern**, not a one-off.
- One quirk worth knowing: your letter cites the **old** de minimis regulation (1407/2013, €200k
  ceiling). The law in force since 1.1.2024 is **Regulation (EU) 2023/2831** — ceiling **€300,000
  per "single undertaking" over a rolling 3 years**. The official Finnish forms were still citing
  the old regulation as late as August 2025; the applied test is the new one.

For your own books: the €400 counts against your company's €300k ceiling (trivially), and you must
declare it whenever you apply for anything else granted as de minimis (starttiraha, Finnvera
guarantees, palkkatuki, Business Finland grants...). Keep the letter 10 years.

## 3. How the money flows (the model, end to end)

```
EU funds (ERDF/ESF+) + Finnish state
        │  granted via call, 60–75% of project costs
        ▼
Granting authority (Uudenmaan liitto / elinvoimakeskus)
        │  funding decision names every aided company + € value  ← the formal "grant"
        ▼
Project implementer(s)  ←— YOU want to be here
        │  runs free workshops; buys trainers, venues, tools, licenses
        │  collects de minimis declarations BEFORE each measure
        ▼
Participating companies (free or subsidized seat)
        │  each receives benefit valued at market price (e.g. €400)
        └─▶ gets a tiedoksianto; value counts against its €300k/3yr ceiling
```

Key mechanics every organizer must internalize:

- **The market price is set by you, approved by the funder.** In the EURA 2021 application you list
  each measure, its market price per company (tip from the funder's own training: benchmark a
  comparable commercial service), the company's payment share, and aid = price − payment.
- **Aid must be granted *before* the workshop happens.** The authority's decision (or a change
  decision adding new companies — done in batches, e.g. per cohort) must precede the measure.
  You cannot run the workshop first and do the paperwork after.
- **Companies with a full ceiling, or who refuse to sign, can still attend — at full price.** Their
  fees count as private financing of your project and reduce your own-funding gap.
- **Open-to-everyone events are not aid.** Only measures for a *selected/limited* group of
  companies create de minimis aid. (The EDIH regime states this even more sharply: high-attendance
  open trainings can be valued at zero.) This is a design lever: open seminars = no paperwork;
  closed cohorts and company-specific work = de minimis machinery.
- **New from 1.1.2026:** every de minimis grant must be reported to the EU's central register
  (eAidRegister) within 20 working days. Finland uses the EU-level register; KEHA-keskus
  administers access. In EURA projects this reporting rides on the granting authority's decision —
  but the register does *not* replace collecting declarations from participants.

## 4. Can you be the organizer? (Eligibility and vehicle choice)

The official eligibility rule for structural-fund projects: the applicant must be a
**julkisoikeudellinen yhteisö tai yksityisoikeudellinen oikeushenkilö** — a public body or a
private-law *legal person*.

| Vehicle | Can lead an EU project? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Toiminimi (your current form) | **No** | Not a legal person. Can only receive *yrityksen kehittämisavustus* for its own development (≤€50k, 50%) |
| Osakeyhtiö (Oy) | **Yes** | Legally eligible; in practice ordinary for-profit micro-companies rarely lead in this niche (funders test "this must not be your normal business / operating aid"; AMKs, cities and associations dominate) |
| Yhdistys (ry) — e.g. AFAES | **Yes** | Associations are standard, credible applicants for ESF+ skills/participation projects, and the "not your commercial business" test is easy to pass |

**Strategic read:** run a two-vehicle play. The **association** is the natural lead applicant for
ESF+/community-facing projects (and Erasmus+); your **company** (incorporated as an Oy when the
volume justifies it) sells trainer services — to your own projects' consortium partners only via
arm's-length procurement (related-party purchases are ineligible unless competitively tendered), and
more importantly to *other* people's machinery: AMK-led projects, FAIR, employment areas, Jotpa.

Other hard requirements to plan for:

- **Cash flow:** support is paid **in arrears** against reported costs (6–12 month cycles). You
  front the money.
- **Own financing:** 25–40% of the budget must come from you/municipalities/private sources
  (participation fees help).
- **Results must be publicly exploitable** — your materials and models get shared. The de minimis
  designation is the legal carve-out that lets *companies* keep their benefit.
- **EURA 2021 admin:** Suomi.fi mandates, per-person time records (in some salary models),
  participant reporting, indicators (e.g. RCO04 "companies receiving de minimis aid").

## 5. The route map — four ways in, ranked by speed

### Route 1 (now): plug into machinery that already has money

- **FAIR — Finnish AI Region EDIH.** Finland's AI-focused European Digital Innovation Hub, renewed
  with top EU scores for a second period from 1.1.2026 (~through 2028), consortium of 10: cities of
  Helsinki & Espoo, Enter Espoo, Aalto, University of Helsinki, Tampere University, Haaga-Helia,
  Metropolia, KIRAHub, 28 Digital. Its remit — free AI services, training, an AI helpdesk, AI Act
  compliance support for SMEs — is exactly your offering. EDIHs may subcontract (~30% of costs) and
  FAIR runs an **associate partner** program (application via fairedih.fi contact form). The
  trainer-procurement route isn't published; approach the operational contact directly
  (kaarina.suominen@hel.fi, City of Helsinki). **No new Finnish EDIH consortium call until ~2028 —
  the existing hubs *are* the market.**
- **AMK-led project consortia.** Haaga-Helia, Metropolia and Laurea run this space (AI-TIE,
  Tekoälyhautomo). Pitch yourself now as *osatoteuttaja* (co-implementer, with your own budget and
  decision) or subcontracted trainer for their **next** application round — consortia form months
  before calls open. Your differentiators: hands-on agentic-AI/coding-tools curriculum, multilingual
  delivery, reach into communities the AMKs don't reach.
- **Municipal small purchases.** Below €60,000, cities can buy training directly without tendering
  (pienhankinta). Espoo and Helsinki business services and city staff-training units are buying AI
  training now (Kuopio already runs staff AI courses).

### Route 2 (0–6 months): sell into publicly funded training procurement

The 2025 TE-reform moved employment services to municipal **työllisyysalueet** (Espoo–Kauniainen,
Helsinki, Vantaa–Kerava...). They procure:

- **Työvoimakoulutus** — free courses for jobseekers, tendered on Hilma / dynamic supplier
  registers that **reopen at least every two years** (e.g. Helsinki's €19.2M register for 55+
  muutosturvakoulutus). Precedent that AI training is bought from private firms: Saranen
  Consulting's "Tekoäly asiantuntijatyössä F.E.C" runs in two Uusimaa employment areas.
- **Yhteishankintakoulutus** — the closest thing to "EU pays for my client's training on demand":
  you bring a corporate client to the employment authority, which co-funds tailored training —
  **RekryKoulutus 70% public / TäsmäKoulutus 50–70% / MuutosKoulutus 80%** (GBER training-aid
  based). The employer proposes; a provider is procured.
- **Jotpa** (Service Centre for Continuous Learning) — funds courses that are **free for
  participants**, explicitly buys from private providers, and made **"AI use in SMEs" a 2026
  priority**. Precedent: Jotpa procured the national free **Tekoälytalkoot** AI courses from
  **MinnaLearn**, a private company. Watch its **October 2026 "myynnin ja kaupan digitalisaatio"**
  procurement. (Risk: the August 2026 budget proposal would abolish its host agency OPH in 2027 —
  final word at the September 2026 budjettiriihi.)
- **Sell through publicly funded educators:** trainer roster / subcontracting with Omnia, Keuda,
  AMKs, työväenopistot and kesäyliopistot (AI courses are booming there; VOS state funding
  subsidizes participant fees; subcontracted statutory education can even be VAT-exempt).

**Set up today:** Hilma (hankintailmoitukset.fi) saved-search alerts — free, up to 20 — on CPV
80500000 (training), 80533100 (computer training), 80400000 (adult education) + keywords
"tekoäly", "digitaidot"; register on tarjouspalvelu.fi and hankintasampo.fi. A 2026 procurement-law
amendment (in force in phases) mandates splitting EU-threshold contracts into lots — deliberately
easier for micro-firms to win pieces.

### Route 3 (2027): lead your own structural-fund project — the full Tekoälyhautomo model

The instrument that matches your ambition exactly: **ESF+ priority 4.2 "Uutta osaamista
työelämään"** (new skills for working life). The 2026 Uusimaa call (closed 7.4.2026): **€2.0M
envelope, max 75% support, projects ≤2.5 years**, target groups explicitly include *solo
entrepreneurs and the self-employed*, employees and managers of SMEs. Requirements worth designing
for from day one: participants must gain **verifiable competence** (osaamismerkit/badges with
assessment — a bare attendance certificate doesn't count), and scoring rewards novel education
solutions and provider–employer cooperation. Uudenmaan liitto has announced **more EAKR and ESF+
calls for Uusimaa in 2026 and 2027** — effectively the last window of this programme period
(projects end by ~2028; the 2028–2034 period's new machinery won't produce calls before ~2028–29).

Realistic first project shape for a small operator:

- **Kertakorvaus (lump sum) model, total ≤ €200,000**: payment against pre-agreed verified outputs,
  minimal financial bureaucracy — built for first-timers (binary delivery risk: output not achieved
  = not paid).
- Or a **ryhmähanke** where you lead a small consortium (funder currently recommends ≤3
  implementers) with, say, one AMK and one municipality/association — instant credibility and
  co-funding.
- Budget alternative for tools-heavy delivery: the **7% flat-rate cost model** (see §6).
- Lighter warm-up: **AKKE** micro-grants from Uudenmaan liitto (~€20–150k rounds) for a pilot.

Funders explicitly invite pre-application contact: eakr@uudenmaanliitto.fi (EAKR/urban development)
and Kaakkois-Suomen elinvoimakeskus (ESF+ for Uusimaa since the 1.1.2026 agency reform replaced
Hämeen ELY).

### Route 4: EU-direct programmes

- **Erasmus+ adult education — the best low-barrier grant for the AFAES angle.**
  **KA210-ADU small-scale partnerships: €30k or €60k lump sum, only 2 organisations from 2
  countries, ~10-page application, explicitly designed for newcomers.** Next deadlines: possibly
  1 Oct 2026 (verify with OPH whether Finland opens a second round), otherwise March 2027; KA220
  (€120k/€250k/€400k) for the bigger version. Finnish companies *and* associations are eligible.
  Caveat: Erasmus+ funds developing methods/materials with international partners, not simply
  paying you to train Finns.
- **Digital Europe Programme skills calls.** Open now: **DIGITAL-2026-SKILLS-10, deadline
  1 October 2026** (€12.5M; incl. AI-in-health training €7.8M). Any Finnish legal entity can join a
  consortium (funding 50%/75%/100% depending on action type); realistic role for you is a small
  work-package partner recruited by a university. The EU **AI Skills Academy (project AISHA**,
  €7M, 2026–2030, 30 beneficiaries) is building accredited AI training — contact about associated
  partnership. High admin; a 2027+ play.
- **Interreg Central Baltic** (Uusimaa eligible, 80% co-financing, small-project format with
  simplified admin) — check whether a sixth call opens; Baltic Sea Region programme is closed for
  this period.

### The community program is a different — easier — game

Your Technology & AI Literacy Program for immigrants serves **individuals**, who are not
"undertakings": **no state aid, no de minimis, no €400 letters.** Relevant money: ESF+ priority 4.3
"Yhdenvertaiseen osallisuuteen" (a call for Southern Finland was open 3.8.–5.10.2026), Jotpa
(courses free for participants by design), Erasmus+ KA210/KA220, city grants (Espoo/Helsinki), STEA,
and delivery partnerships with työväenopistot. AFAES ry is the natural applicant everywhere here.

## 6. Paying for tools, subscriptions and tokens with project money

The specific question — can the project buy participants ChatGPT/Claude/Copilot subscriptions and
API credits ("tokens")? **Yes, structured correctly:**

**Cost-model choice decides everything (fixed for the whole project):**

| Model | How tool costs are treated |
|---|---|
| **Flat rate 40%** (default) | Only salaries are itemized; *everything else* — licenses, tokens, trainers, venues — must fit inside an automatic +40% of salaries. No receipts, but also no extra money. Fine if tool costs are small |
| **Flat rate 7%** | Salaries + **itemized direct costs**: content-related purchased services (external trainers; SaaS subscriptions and API usage bought for the target group), materials, equipment ≤€3,000/item, venues rented for the target group. Available when purchased services ≥30% of salaries — i.e., built for exactly your case |
| **Kertakorvaus** (≤€200k) | Costs pre-agreed in the budget, payment on outputs — tools priced in from the start |

**How to structure the benefit (the EDIH template):** maintain a **price list** (market price or
full cost + reasonable margin) for each package — e.g. "AI coding workshop series incl. 3-month
Claude Pro + €50 API credits: €650/company." Aid per company = list price − what they pay. Value it
identically in the application, the funding decision, the tiedoksianto and the register.

**Guardrails found in the rules and funder guidance:**

- Scope subscriptions/credits to the **training period**. Costs of a participant's ordinary
  business are ineligible, and anything resembling a cash-equivalent voucher is a red flag
  (vouchers are expressly ineligible in regional-council projects).
- **Procurement duties** when buying with grant money: ≥€3,000 → document the ordinary price
  level; ≥€10,000 → request an adequate number of tenders; ≥€60,000 (national threshold) →
  hankintalaki tendering. Don't split purchases artificially; related-party buys need competition.
- **VAT:** eligible as a cost only if you can show you can't deduct/recover it. Whether the
  per-company benefit value includes VAT has no single published answer (Business Finland practice
  points to VAT-exclusive for VAT-registered recipients) — fix the basis with the funder in writing.
- **Precedents:** no published Finnish EURA project has yet itemized AI subscriptions/tokens as the
  de minimis benefit — closest models are EDIH service price-lists and EU projects handing out GPU
  hours and API credits (SECURED, AI-BOOST; LUMI AI Factory offers SMEs free compute). Novel ≠
  ineligible: the funder decks generically allow "free or underpriced services and use of
  development environments" — but agree the treatment with the funder before you promise it.

An alternative aid basis exists — **GBER Article 31 training aid** (50–70% intensity, so
participants co-pay 30–50%) — used in Finland for yhteishankintakoulutus. For free-to-participant
workshops, de minimis remains the standard and simpler channel; Art. 31 is a fallback for
participants whose €300k ceiling is full.

## 7. Your obligations when you're the organizer (the compliance loop)

For every *closed-group* measure benefiting companies:

1. **Before anything happens:** collect a signed **de minimis declaration** from each company
   (prior aid in the 3-year window, group structure; or the "I decline aid and pay full price"
   option). Exclusions to screen: primary agriculture, fishery/aquaculture; mixed-sector companies
   need separated accounts.
2. **Get it granted:** companies + measure + market price go into the funding application or a
   **muutoshakemus** (batch new cohorts). The authority's decision *is* the grant — and it must
   predate the workshop.
3. **Deliver and document the tiedoksianto** (the letter you received) to every aided company;
   confirm delivery in each payment application.
4. **Report:** measures in the monitoring report; indicator "companies receiving de minimis aid";
   from 1.1.2026 grants flow to the EU central register (via the authority for EURA projects — the
   exact operational split was still being clarified by TEM in Dec 2025).
5. **Archive 10 years.** No sanction is prescribed for late registration, but unregistered aid can
   become *unlawful* — recoverable from the participant with interest. That's the reputational
   nightmare to design against.

Sizing note: at €400/company, even a 100-company program distributes €40k of aid — nowhere near
anyone's €300k ceiling. The machinery is bureaucratic, not restrictive, at workshop scale.

## 8. Market context: why demand exists (and how to pitch it honestly)

- **EU AI Act Article 4** obliges providers and deployers of AI systems to ensure staff AI literacy
  — in application since 2 Feb 2025, national supervision from Aug 2026. **Important:** the July
  2026 Digital Omnibus (Reg. 2026/1744) softened it to a duty to "support the development of" AI
  literacy, explicitly not guaranteeing any specific level. Pitch AI literacy as risk management +
  productivity with a regulatory tailwind — not "mandatory certified training" (anyone selling it
  that way is now overstating the law).
- **OKM published Finland's national AI competence framework for citizens (11.5.2026)** — four
  levels: understand, evaluate, apply. No money attached yet, but it will likely become the spec
  for publicly funded AI literacy. **Map your curriculum onto it now**; when funding follows the
  framework, you're pre-aligned.
- The Commission's stated support channel for SME AI literacy is **free EDIH training** — i.e.,
  public policy is actively routing money to exactly the delivery role you want.
- **Free-tier competition:** Elements of AI, MinnaLearn's Tekoälytalkoot, AIStart cohorts. Your
  offer must sit above these: hands-on builder skills (agents, coding tools, automations),
  multilingual delivery, underserved communities — which is precisely your existing plan.
- **Instruments that no longer exist** (don't build on folklore): ELY yritysten kehittämispalvelut
  (analysis/consulting/training subsidies) — abolished; Business Finland innovaatioseteli — closed
  10/2024; koulutusvähennys tax deduction — ended 2025; employer koulutuskorvaus — ended 2026. The
  ELY centres themselves became **elinvoimakeskukset** on 1.1.2026.

- **Cash flow** (payments in arrears) and **own financing** (25–40%) are the micro-operator's
  hardest constraints — mitigate via kertakorvaus, consortium roles, and participation fees.
- **Call timing risk:** the 2027 Uusimaa rounds are announced in principle, not scheduled; the
  programme period ends — after that, the 2028–2034 architecture (national partnership plans, more
  defence/competitiveness emphasis) is genuinely uncertain.
- **Jotpa/OPH institutional future** decided at the Sept 2026 budjettiriihi.
- **No precedent** yet for itemized AI-token benefits in Finnish projects — get written funder
  sign-off early.

  now cites 2023/2831; whether OPH opens an autumn KA210 round; FAIR's actual subcontracting
