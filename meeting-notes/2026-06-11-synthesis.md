### Lessons learned from previous editions (what to fix)

- **Never depend on a partner for the venue.** The embassies promised a venue for two years and never delivered; the team had to scramble three weeks out and pay for a place themselves. Conclusion: control the critical path, treat partner promises as unconfirmed until contracted.
- Partner failures degrade the whole event (e.g., food vendor cancelled 24h before Africa Day) → **every critical element needs a contingency plan**.
- Planning a few months out caused all of this; planning 12 months out removes the excuses.
- Communication failures with freelancers (videographer saga, see §6): from now on, every engagement gets a **clear framework, written brief, reference examples, and deadlines** up front.
- People who attend "in name only" (present but not engaged) hurt delivery; build the team from people who take ownership, and be willing to bring in fresh people.

### Positioning ambition

- Explicit goal: **Slush-level quality** — become the event partners *ask* to join ("Slush tells you to come, you show up"). Position Africa Week so partners and embassies need to come find a spot, not the other way around.
- The brand is bigger than "jumping and dancing": business, technology, academia, innovation — "a different side of Africa."

## 6. Video production post-mortem (lessons for all future content work)

A freelance videographer engagement went badly: missed deadlines, didn't follow the agreed framework (4 videos: one 3–4 min main video + 1-min recipe videos), didn't use the second camera's footage or the reference videos/edit plan provided, required daily chasing.

Codified lessons:
- Give every creative a **framework, written script/edit plan, reference videos, and deadlines** — which was done — but also verify they have their own working system before committing.
- Footage is expensive and unrepeatable; the person filming owns the details on the day.
- If someone can't deliver, they should say so early — plenty of people want gigs.
- Be explicit about roles: a blogger ≠ an event reporter; ask people directly what they want to do ("my passion is technology") so you can build around them.
- The team member who plans scripts meticulously and executes fast is the model to standardize on; flexibility is the trait to demand.
