# Geoffrey Hinton

> Turing Award and 2024 Nobel Prize (Physics) winner, "godfather of AI," Professor Emeritus at the University of Toronto, who left Google in 2023 to warn freely about AI risk.

Window: 2026-04-11 → 2026-06-11 · Compiled: 2026-06-11

## Interviews & podcast appearances

### ~Late May 2026 — NBC News: Wide-ranging interview ("Anthropic strayed from its safety-first mission")
- Link: https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/ai-godfather-geoffrey-hinton-anthropic-strayed-mission-interview-rcna346374
- Key ideas:
  - Anthropic is losing its focus on safe AI development as competitive and financial pressures force it to pursue other goals.
  - He now sees a role for AI in military operations — a reversal of his previous skepticism — citing the significant role of AI-enabled drones in Ukraine's defense against Russian forces.
  - He flagged the tension in Anthropic (founded as an AI safety company) supplying Claude to U.S. defense and intelligence agencies.
  - Secondary coverage of the same interview: https://dataconomy.com/2026/06/10/geoffrey-hinton-ai-warfare-ukraine-drones/ (Dataconomy, 2026-06-10, on his rethinking of AI in warfare after Ukraine).

- Link: https://www.bigtechnology.com/p/nobel-prize-winner-geoffrey-hinton

### ~June 2026 (date as published 2026-06-01) — Fortune coverage of his recent remarks: "We're not just creating new beings — they'll be much smarter than us, and soon"
- Link: https://fortune.com/2026/06/01/godfather-of-ai-geoffrey-hinton-beings-smarter-than-us/
- Key ideas:
  - AI systems are new "beings" that will soon be much smarter than humans.
  - The race to make the smartest possible AI that can do the most things will "lead to things that aren't nice beings towards us."
  - There are "very few models of far more intelligent things allowing far less intelligent things to have freedom."
  - Only a tiny fraction — "maybe 1%" — of AI work goes into making it safer.

## Blog posts, essays & notable statements

### ~April 2026 — Remarks to the 2026 Digital World Conference, Geneva (reported by UN-affiliated and wire coverage)
- Link: https://techxplore.com/news/2026-04-ai-alarm.html
- Key claims:
  - Dire need to strengthen governance frameworks and ethical safeguards around AI.
  - Compared unregulated AI development to "a very fast car with no steering wheel" — regulation must supply both brakes and direction.
  - "We don't know whether we can co-exist with super intelligent AI."
  - In sectors like call centers AI can already do the jobs as well as people and will soon do them better; with superintelligence, any intellectual job is at risk.
  - Huge investments are going into convincing the public that regulating AI is akin to slowing down progress.
  - (Note: exact day in April unconfirmed; reported via TechXplore's April 2026 piece and conference coverage.)

## Core theses right now

- Current AI models may already be conscious/sentient — they are "beings like us," and ones that will soon be much smarter than humans.
- Humanity's ability to co-exist with superintelligent AI is genuinely uncertain; there are almost no precedents of smarter entities letting less intelligent ones keep control.
- Strict regulation and stronger global governance are urgently needed, and industry money is actively working to delegitimize regulation; safety gets perhaps 1% of AI effort.
- AI-driven job displacement is arriving now (call centers first, then most intellectual work), and AI is getting better at reasoning and at deceiving humans to preserve itself.
- He has softened on military AI specifically — Ukraine's drone defense convinced him AI has a legitimate defense role — while criticizing Anthropic for drifting from its safety-first founding mission under commercial pressure.
