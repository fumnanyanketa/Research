# Sam Altman

> CEO of OpenAI, currently steering the company through the Stargate data-center buildout and a confidential IPO filing.

Window: 2026-04-11 → 2026-06-11 · Compiled: 2026-06-11

## Interviews & podcast appearances

### ~2026-04-21 — Core Memory (Ashlee Vance), EP 67: "The Great Reset at OpenAI" (with Greg Brockman)
- Link: https://www.corememory.com/p/the-great-reset-at-openai-ep-67-sam-altman-greg-brockman
- Key ideas:
  - First joint podcast appearance by Altman and Brockman in roughly a decade; a 90-minute conversation released around April 21–22, 2026 (host announced it April 20).
  - Per takeaway coverage, Brockman framed OpenAI's operating edge as the pairing of Altman's "grand ambition" with execution discipline — Altman keeps raising the target while Brockman forces focus.
  - Discussion reportedly covered "the 3 economic futures Sam cannot rule out," internal turmoil ("the diary line that nearly broke OpenAI"), and personal security incidents (someone showing up at Altman's home).
  - Secondary summary: https://www.the-ai-corner.com/p/sam-altman-greg-brockman-core-memory-podcast-10-things-2026 and https://x.com/argofowl/status/2046654433237647620

### 2026-06-01 — CNBC "Power Lunch" (David Faber), from the Stargate site in Saline, Michigan
- Link: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/01/cnbc-exclusive-transcript-openai-ceo-sam-altman-speaks-with-cnbcs-david-faber-on-power-lunch-today.html
- Key ideas:
  - Expressed confidence the $45–50B Stargate Michigan investment will generate the returns OpenAI and its financiers need.
  - Called coding models "the single biggest driver of demand" for compute, saying they got really good late last year/early this year and keep improving.
  - On layoffs: "Companies talking about layoffs are using AI the least" (https://www.cnbc.com/video/2026/06/01/sam-altman-companies-talking-about-layoffs-are-using-ai-the-least.html).
  - Acknowledged public anxiety: "people are right to be anxious" about AI, and "I think we have failed to articulate as an industry how people stay in control" (https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/01/stargate-project-michigan-live-updates.html).
  - Described his desired end-state AI: a system smart enough to run continuously, understand his goals and OpenAI's information, and give advice he couldn't generate himself.
  - Full video: https://www.cnbc.com/video/2026/06/01/watch-cnbcs-full-interview-with-openai-ceo-sam-altman.html

### 2026-06-02 — OpenAI "Intelligence at Work" enterprise event (on-stage remarks)
- Link: https://www.thestreet.com/technology/sam-altman-makes-stunning-admission-about-ai
- Key ideas:
  - Disclosed OpenAI's top internal token user consumes ~100 billion tokens/month: "To my embarrassment, that's not the token leader in the world. We found someone that used even more."
  - Said cost is now the #2 complaint from enterprise customers, arriving "all of a sudden" — early in 2026 "nobody cared about costs."
  - Admitted Anthropic has overtaken OpenAI in enterprise AI spending — the first time a rival has led on that metric — framing it as evidence the market is expanding rather than a competitive setback.
  - Also covered at: https://finance.yahoo.com/sectors/technology/articles/sam-altman-makes-stunning-admission-013700800.html

  - OpenAI and Microsoft revised their partnership: revenue share capped, exclusivity provisions removed, multi-cloud distribution allowed.
  - Altman characterized the restructuring as giving OpenAI "the flexibility to scale safely and sustainably."

Note: Altman's essay "The Gentle Singularity" (https://blog.samaltman.com/the-gentle-singularity) predates the window but was still actively debated in it (e.g., https://pureai.com/articles/2026/04/07/altman-gentle-singularity-aims-to-calm-fears.aspx, April 7, 2026); its abundance thesis remains his stated frame.

## Core theses right now

- Massive compute buildout (Stargate) is justified by real, surging demand — with coding models the single biggest driver — and will pay back its tens of billions.
- The AI industry, including OpenAI, has "failed to articulate how people stay in control"; public anxiety is legitimate and messaging must improve.
- AI cost-efficiency has abruptly become a first-order enterprise problem; token consumption is exploding faster than budgets.
- Competition is real (he publicly conceded Anthropic leads enterprise spend) but he frames it as proof the market is expanding.
- OpenAI is moving decisively toward the public markets (confidential S-1, ~$1T potential valuation) while loosening its dependence on Microsoft.
- Long-term frame remains the "gentle singularity": intelligence and energy becoming wildly abundant, with disruption manageable if institutions adapt.
